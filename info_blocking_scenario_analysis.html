Scenario A
Paper Records & the Infeasibility Exception
A patient asks her primary care provider to send 10 years of medical records to a research study she's enrolled in. The records include paper documents that would need to be scanned. The practice manager says it will take 6 weeks due to the volume and staff capacity. She offers the patient digital records from the past 3 years (already in the EHR) within 2 business days.
Question
Does the 6-week timeline for paper records likely constitute information blocking?
B
Scenario B
Vendor Contract & Contractual Information Blocking
A hospital's EHR vendor sends a contract renewal that includes the following clause: "Customer shall not use any third-party software or APIs to extract data from the System without prior written approval from Vendor." The hospital's CISO is concerned but says the clause is standard.
Question
Is this contract clause permissible under the 21st Century Cures Act?

In practice, health informaticists need to audit both their vendor relationships and internal records workflows to surface information blocking risks before they become compliance violations. The two most common failure modes are exactly what you've described: contractual restrictions that limit data access, and operational delays that exceed what the Infeasibility exception permits. Strong governance requires regular contract review cycles and documented workflows with defined turnaround standards.
